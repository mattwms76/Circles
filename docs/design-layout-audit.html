Circles · current layout audit · 16 October 2026

Keep the visual identity. Give content more room.

Circles has a recognizable palette, readable body text and clear primary buttons. The current layout spends too much of the first screen on controls, headings and decorative space. The next design pass should improve hierarchy and consistency while preserving accessible text and touch targets.

Review basis: the running browser prototype at 127.0.0.1:4174, observed screenshots/accessibility state and DOM measurements; React Native source comparison. This audit created documentation only.

Implementation update: all ten findings have been addressed. See the implementation and verification record. The observations below preserve the original audit.

L01 · P1Confirmed preview defect

Profile Settings labels exceed their button width

Expanding Settings leaves its actions constrained to a narrow toolbar column. “Notification preferences” and “Find circles for my interests” visibly extend beyond their button boundaries.

Measured evidence: both buttons were approximately 120 px wide, with 119 px client widths and scroll widths of 136 and 146 px. Their text uses white-space: nowrap. Catch-up preferences occupies another column, reducing the available space further.

Recommended change: give Settings a full-width sheet or menu with wrapping, left-aligned rows. Keep the profile toolbar compact rather than expanding complex settings inside a column.

Acceptance: labels remain inside their controls at 320/390 px and enlarged text; expansion does not collide with neighboring actions. Treat this as a prototype defect until the separate native Settings layout is tested.

Sources: profileToolbar markup, profileToolbar and button rules. Native Settings uses its own Sheet/Disclosure structure in CommunityScreens.

L02 · P1

Home gives more initial space to controls than posts

The initial view stacks the brand, greeting, empty story rail, explanatory copy, two quick actions, timeline title, Saved posts, Recommended/Latest, Catch up, another explanation and Manage timeline preferences. These are individually useful; together they delay the feed.

Measured evidence: at a 390 × 844 browser viewport the first post started at y=704 and bottom navigation at y=766, leaving only 62 px of its header visible. At 320 × 700 the post started at y=875, below navigation at y=629. The empty story section occupied 169–187 px. These are browser coordinates including preview chrome, not physical-device measurements.

Recommended change: make the empty story rail compact; retain a recognizable create-story entry. Combine feed mode and timeline actions into one compact row, move preference management behind an icon/menu, and shorten explanatory copy after first use. Keep one clear create-post action.

Acceptance: at the agreed standard mobile size, the first post's author and first body line are visible before scrolling. At narrow/large-text sizes, preserve reading and touch sizes and allow graceful reflow rather than shrinking text to achieve that goal.

Sources: observed Home screenshots/geometry; Home ListHeaderComponent, Stories, FeedControls.

L03 · P1

Circle tools compete with the active section

Posts includes circle identity, three section tabs, Write a post, an empty stories disclosure, Search and filters, Ask or poll, Circle library, Circle chat and Community tools before post content. Ask/poll/library/chat/tools also appear above Events and About, even when the user is looking for event or rules information.

Recommended change: use a compact persistent circle header. Give the active section its own primary action; place chat/library and other community destinations in a stable secondary navigation or one Tools route. Keep host controls role-specific. Collapse or omit empty story space without obscuring story creation.

Acceptance: Posts shows content promptly; Events shows the next event or its empty-state action before unrelated tools; About puts description/rules first. All existing tools remain discoverable without being repeated in every section.

Sources: observed Posts, Events and About; CircleDetailScreen renders CircleFeatures for all member sections; CircleFeatures.

L04 · P2

Explore cards are expensive in vertical space

After the title, search, interest chips, Filters and section heading, the preview displays approximately one discovery card. A large pastel rectangle with a small emoji dominates that card. Description, membership, Join/Joined, Open circle and recommendation text add further height.

Recommended change: test a compact row/card with a small cover thumbnail, name, short description, membership/privacy metadata and one unambiguous join action. Reserve larger editorial cards for deliberately featured circles. Provide a clipped-edge/fade cue for the horizontally scrolling interest row.

Acceptance: users can compare more than one candidate in the discovery area at the standard mobile size without losing descriptive text or usable targets. Opening a circle and joining it have separate, predictable focus/tap regions.

Sources: circleCard uses an emoji hero and a button-role card containing a Join button. Native CircleTile already separates pressable regions and uses real images; the empty pastel hero is a preview-specific representation, not proof of a native image failure.

L05 · P2

Message options and attachments increase conversation height

In the preview, every short bubble has a separate timestamp/options row, including a 36 px-high menu target. The composer adds a full-width Photo or voice note button below the text field. This makes brief exchanges feel much larger than their content.

Recommended change: keep timestamps quiet and group them where sensible; expose message actions through one consistently accessible interaction without adding an options row to every bubble. Put an attachment trigger beside the composer and expand media controls on demand. Keep Send distinct and preserve room for multi-line drafts.

Acceptance: short messages remain compact; every action is keyboard/screen-reader reachable; media selection, reply context and error feedback do not obscure the input. Verify with the real keyboard, long drafts, voice recording and large OS text.

Sources: messageMeta/messageMenu/threadComposer. Native ChatScreen already uses tap-to-open message actions and a separate MessageMediaComposer plus a hint above the input. Do not introduce the preview's oversized per-bubble footer into native.

L06 · P2

Secondary actions need a quieter hierarchy

Outlined rounded rectangles are used for feed choices, navigation, utilities, media choices and status-like controls. Around the timeline and circle tools, many equally weighted controls compete. Post media choices use a regular two-column icon grid, while story media choices use uneven text-only wrapping.

Recommended change: use one primary action per task, a quiet secondary style for utilities, and a clear segmented/tab treatment for mutually exclusive views. Standardize the attachment chooser across posts, stories and messages while retaining different duration rules. Keep destructive actions distinct and away from everyday creation actions.

Acceptance: each screen has an obvious next action; selected, loading, disabled and error states remain distinguishable. Long labels wrap or change layout rather than overflowing. Consolidation preserves target size.

Sources: ActionButton/Pill/MediaButton, post composer, story composer, prototype controls.

L07 · P2

Profile mixes identity, administration and community browsing

The preview combines Change photo, Edit profile, Activity, Settings, Catch-up preferences, Following and Followers ahead of community content. Following and Followers are separate full-width disclosure rows. This makes the profile feel like a settings page before it feels like a person's community space.

Recommended change: keep the avatar, name, handle and bio as one identity block with Edit profile as the main owner action. Group follower/following counts compactly while preserving access to their lists. Move account preferences to Settings and organize personal content/circles with clear sections. Wallet should remain a separate secondary destination.

Acceptance: a member can distinguish editing identity, viewing community activity and managing the account. Profile navigation does not create narrow expanding toolbar columns. Long names/bios and empty membership states remain readable.

Sources: observed profile; ProfileScreen, profile enhancements, catch-up/community additions.

L08 · P1

Prototype and native layouts have diverged

The browser uses a parallel HTML/CSS implementation. Its outgoing messages are lilac/dark, while native uses the coral primary color. Its conversation options footer differs from native tap actions. Discovery uses emoji hero blocks in the preview and image-backed cards in native. Several native screens include a TopBar omitted from their corresponding prototype screens.

Recommended change: agree a screen-by-screen design inventory and align structure, spacing tokens, control types and fixtures. Select native screenshots as release evidence and keep prototype labels explicit. Consolidate competing late CSS overrides before adding another layer of layout rules.

Acceptance: a design change is checked in both implementations against the same representative states. Prototype visual tests cannot be mistaken for proof of native keyboard, text-scaling, safe-area or interaction behavior.

Sources: messageMine and native layout styles, native screens, multiple message/profile overrides, prototype visual coverage.

L09 · P2

Empty states and sheets need a consistent space strategy

The empty Replies view uses a large bordered conversation area containing a single “No comments yet” line. Post and story sheets use different media-control arrangements and substantially different open heights. Preferences and wallet are comparatively clear and should guide simpler sheets.

Recommended change: use a compact or centered empty state that makes the next step clear; avoid a large card whose only purpose is blank space. Define short action-sheet, medium form-sheet and full conversation/editor patterns, with a stable header and reachable submit/composer area.

Acceptance: empty, populated, loading and error views use intentional space. Long forms can scroll while the primary action remains reachable. The keyboard never obscures the focused input or required action. Short sheets do not inherit unnecessary conversation-height containers.

Sources: observed Replies, post/story creation, preferences and wallet; native Sheet already includes safe-area and keyboard handling. Actual keyboard coverage still requires devices.

L10 · P3

Normalize navigation icon proportions

The Live glyph appears much smaller than Home/Explore/Inbox/Profile in the inactive state, while Live receives a filled capsule in its selected state. Other destinations mainly change color. This gives the bar uneven visual weight.

Recommended change: align icon optical size, stroke and baseline; use one selected-state pattern unless Live is intentionally a promoted creation action. Keep labels visible and preserve the existing accessible target areas.

Acceptance: each destination has equivalent legibility and selection clarity in light/dark and large text. A decorative icon size change does not reduce the actual touch target.

Sources: observed bottom navigation; tab SVG styles specify different Live glyph dimensions; TabBar/TabGlyph and native navigation styling require optical comparison on device.

Spacing, typography, color and controls

Preserve the existing typography scale

Measured preview post text is 16 px with 24 px line height; metadata is 14/20. Native theme tokens already define body 16/24, secondary 14/20 and caption 12/16. These are useful foundations. Do not reduce body text or controls to compensate for the crowded first screen. Limit stronger weights to headings, names, selected states and unread content. Preview timestamps use 11 px, so check their legibility separately from body text.

Use consistent spacing to distinguish groups

The native spacing scale already includes 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 and 32. Prefer 20 px page gutters, 8–12 px within a related group and 20–24 px between sections, adapting to native platform needs. Remove duplicate wrappers/margins around circle tools rather than removing all breathing room. Match preview padding to the approved native layout.

The sampled text palette is healthy

Calculated from shared tokens, ordinary white text on primary is 5.23:1; muted text on the surface is 6.33:1 in light and 7.87:1 in dark. These samples exceed the 4.5:1 ordinary-text benchmark. This is not a complete contrast certification: images, gradients, alpha blending, focus rings and every state were not measured.

Retain the warm light palette, dark surfaces and coral emphasis. Use lilac/sage/butter as restrained grouping accents. Low-contrast disabled controls are not automatically a contrast failure; they still need a clear explanation of what enables the action.

Protect touch and focus while simplifying

Many native controls already use 48 px minimum dimensions, and preview primary controls commonly measure 48 px high. Keep usable targets when reducing visual weight. WCAG's web 24 × 24 CSS px minimum criterion includes exceptions and spacing conditions; it should not be confused with a claim that every mobile control should be reduced to that size. Keyboard focus order, icon labels and selected states need behavioral checks alongside screenshots.

Suggested implementation order

  1. Fix the confirmed overflow and agree native parity: L01, L08. Establish common screen/state references before broad styling changes.
  2. Improve the first screen of content: L02, L03. Compact empty stories and secondary controls; prioritize timeline/events/rules.
  3. Improve browsing and conversation: L04, L05, L07. Compact discovery, clean up the composer and organize profile destinations.
  4. Unify component behavior and polish: L06, L09, L10. Standardize sheets, attachment choices, secondary actions and icon proportions.
  5. Verify the final layouts: actual light/dark screenshots; 320/390 px prototype checks; native large text, safe areas, keyboard, VoiceOver/TalkBack, loading/errors and long names. Include an assertion for a control's own text overflow, not only page-wide overflow.

The existing Playwright suite checks page/modal horizontal overflow, but L01 demonstrates that a button's label can overflow even when the overall page passes. Add focused checks for the reproduced case when implementing its fix.

This audit elaborates the design work in A05 of the broader action plan; it does not replace the release, safety or operational work in that plan.

Scope and limits

  • Visual observations were made on the actual running preview in its System appearance, which rendered dark. Standard and narrow browser overrides of 390 × 844 and 320 × 700 were checked, and the original viewport was restored afterward.
  • Light palette contrast was calculated from source. No claim is made that every light-mode screen was visually reviewed.
  • The native app was reviewed in source. No signed native screenshots, real keyboard, Dynamic Type, screen-reader session or physical-device recording was available for this audit.
  • No content was posted, uploaded or purchased, and no preferences were toggled. Navigation changed local preview state; no real account or backend data was edited.
  • This is a layout/design audit, not a functional or security certification. Unvisited administrative, onboarding, authentication and less common community states still require the final screen/state inventory.
  • App source was unchanged. This report is the only new project artifact from the review.
