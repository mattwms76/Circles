Circles · fresh design review · 16 October 2026

A sound visual foundation, with ten targeted improvements.

The current app is clearer than the earlier layout audit: Home exposes post content, discovery cards are compact, Profile settings no longer expand into narrow columns, and replies use less empty space. Keep that structure. The most urgent remaining problems concern clipped conversation tools, enlarged-text reflow and invisible filter selection.

Review basis: 44 captured preview states, both main-screen palettes, a 390 × 844 viewport, a 320 × 700 enlarged-text sample, and the React Native screen/component source. This report documents findings; app code was not changed during this review.

D01 · P1Observed preview

Give conversation tools enough visible space

Opening the direct-message options shows a thin scrolling strip instead of a usable menu. Search, mute and explanatory text are difficult to reach, even though they exist in the DOM.

Measured: the sticky conversation header is 48 px high; .conversationToolsBody renders at about 33 px high with 200 px of scrolling content. Its absolute positioning and max-height:55% are resolved against the header context, not the conversation area.

Change: anchor a popover to the conversation panel, use a content-sized action sheet, or render tools in normal flow. Keep the title, close control and composer visible. Preserve the current draft and reply context.

Acceptance: search and mute are visibly reachable at 320/390 px and enlarged text; keyboard focus is not clipped; no tiny nested scrollbar. Assert the visible menu dimensions and usable controls, rather than only checking that the menu exists.

Sources: preview-layout.css, .conversationToolsBody at line 580; preview-experience.js, conversation header/menu construction around lines 378–390. Native ChatScreen uses an in-flow tools area, so the measured clipping is a preview defect.

D02 · P1Observed preview; native checks pending

Make enlarged text reflow without squeezing words into fragments

At 320 × 700 with the preview’s 140% setting, the Timeline options text reappears beside the feed tabs. “Recommended” is squeezed into fragments down a narrow column. Explore cards leave little width for names; the Live hosting callout becomes dominated by a long vertical stack of words.

Measured: the feed toolbar grows to roughly 286 px high. Its tab area is only 49 px wide, while the options button takes about 211 px. The global enlarged-text rule replaces the icon-only button’s font-size:0 with 22 px. It also assigns 22 px to different typography roles rather than scaling each role by 1.4.

Change: keep accessible icon labels visually hidden independently of font size. Let whole control groups wrap or stack before reducing their text width. At narrow widths or large text, move Join/Go live actions beneath card content as needed. Replace the blanket font override with proportional text-role scaling.

Native follow-up: inspect non-wrapping avatar/photo, authentication-button and composer rows in AppScreens/appStyles. These are source risks, not reproduced device failures.

Acceptance: at 100%, 140% and 200% test sizes, labels wrap at words, icons retain their intended size, controls remain reachable and the page does not require horizontal scrolling. Verify native Dynamic Type separately.

Sources: preview-layout.css feed toolbar at lines 120–158 and enlarged-text rules at lines 702–718; LayoutControls; AppScreens; appStyles.

D03 · P1Observed preview

Make selected filters unambiguous

“All conversations” is selected, but it looks the same as “Unread” in the current Inbox. Assistive state is present; the visual state is missing. The onboarding choices demonstrate a better pattern by including a checkmark.

Measured in dark mode: both filters use rgb(36,34,41) background, rgb(244,239,233) text and rgb(65,59,70) border despite opposite aria-pressed values. The same missing distinction is visible in light mode.

Change: resolve the CSS specificity conflict and use a shared selected treatment: checkmark or underline plus an appropriate fill/border. Apply it consistently to Inbox, interests, activity, polls and gift categories. Native selected Pills use the night colour, which is only about 1.01:1 against the dark surface; choose a clearer selected treatment there too.

Acceptance: the selected option is immediately identifiable in both themes and remains distinguishable without depending on hue alone. Assert selected/unselected computed styles and capture both states.

Sources: preview-design.css, .inboxToolbar [aria-pressed='true'] around line 1260 and more specific shared button rules; preview-experience.js Inbox toolbar; Pill.

D04 · P2Measured palette

Separate decorative borders from control boundaries

The warm paper, white surfaces and coral actions work well. Text contrast is generally strong. The same very subtle border token is also used for empty text fields, outlines and other controls, making their boundaries faint.

Calculated from shared tokens: light border/surface is 1.25:1, light border/paper 1.17:1, dark border/surface 1.45:1, and dark border/paper 1.65:1. By comparison, white text on primary is 5.23:1; muted text on surface is 6.33:1 light and 7.87:1 dark.

Change: retain subtle decorative dividers, but add dedicated control-border, focus, selected and destructive tokens. Strengthen empty-input outlines and focus states. Test white labels over actual Live images separately and use a stable scrim or backplate where necessary.

Acceptance: essential visual information identifying controls/states meets the applicable 3:1 non-text contrast criterion. This is not a requirement for every decorative border or every text-labelled button. Preserve the existing readable text palette against the text contrast guidance.

Sources: design-tokens.json; shared field/outline styles in appStyles, CommunityScreens and Experience. Rounded ratios are descriptive measurements, not a conformance certification.

D05 · P2Observed and native source

Use one icon vocabulary and predictable touch areas

Navigation and media controls use drawn icons, while post actions, attachment triggers, back/close controls and live controls mix Unicode symbols and emoji. Stroke, alignment and optical weight vary with platform fonts. A plain circle is a weak visual substitute for a reply bubble.

Source evidence: native Like/Reply/Share glyphs are standalone Text elements with font sizes but no explicit theme colour. Native live controls use microphone emoji, circles and rotation glyphs alongside the custom Icon component. In the preview the Circle chats disclosure measures about 21 px high, below the app’s usual 48 px control height.

Change: extend the existing icon system for reply, heart, share, search, back, close, microphone, camera and attachment. Use explicit theme colours and a common 24 px optical box. Keep decorative emoji for content/gifts. Give icon/disclosure controls consistent target areas without enlarging the glyph itself.

Acceptance: icons have comparable weight and baseline, remain legible in both themes, and have meaningful accessible names. Use 48 px targets as the app design standard, with appropriate platform handling. The web 24 px minimum criterion has spacing and other exceptions; the 21 px measurement alone is not a full WCAG failure assessment.

Sources: Icon, IconButton and PostCard, particularly post action glyphs around lines 407–437; LiveRoom controls around lines 2340–2380; Inbox Circle chats disclosure in preview-depth.js.

D06 · P2Design-system consistency

Apply the existing type scale by meaning

The system font is appropriate and does not need replacing. Body text at 16/24 is comfortable. The inconsistency lies in roles: member names, handles, captions, live controls and safety/helper information often share 12 px styling, while equivalent form labels vary between 14, 15 and 16 px. Some inline Text styles omit font size or line height.

Change: keep one system font stack; use 16/24 for body, 14/20 for helper text and standard labels, 16–17 px for important member names, and 12/16 for genuinely secondary timestamps/badges. Reconcile 22 px section headings and 28–32 px page headings. Use shared tokens rather than adding screen-specific sizes.

Acceptance: each text role has one documented style; explanatory or action-critical copy is not demoted to tiny captions. Long names, localisation and scaling retain a readable hierarchy. Do not shrink body text to fit more controls.

Sources: typography tokens; followerName/followerHandle, profileHandle, replyAuthor and live-control styles; experienceStyles.hint; media editor inline Text. This is a consistency/readability recommendation, not a claim that all 12 px text fails accessibility.

D07 · P2Observed and native source

Keep section navigation stable and use a clear spacing rhythm

Circle Posts/Events use compact identity headers, while About introduces a larger image hero before the same tabs. The tab row changes vertical position just because a section changes. The preview also keeps Search and filters on a separate row from Circle tools, while native has already moved search into those tools.

Change: keep the compact circle identity and tabs stable across sections; place the larger cover inside About content below the tabs. Align page gutters to 20 px, use 8–12 px within related controls and 20–24 px between sections. Review combined wrapper gaps/margins and right-align post overflow actions within their card header.

Acceptance: switching Posts/Events/About does not move the tabs unnecessarily. Page, card and sheet text align intentionally. Related actions form a group instead of separate isolated rows. Preserve the content visibility achieved in the earlier pass.

Sources: CircleDetailScreen, conditional About ImageBackground around lines 1063–1110; circlePageHero/cover and section spacing; circleScreen; circle tools; circle routes/layout.

D08 · P2Visual hierarchy

Distinguish choosing, configuring and submitting

The compact primary screens are improved, but some secondary flows still use many large, similarly weighted buttons. The gift preview gives every item a full-width gift button plus a full-width Favourite button, so only a few choices are visible. Post options show saving, safety and feed preferences in one long list. Native Settings adds an intermediate “Notifications, safety and account” destination before its detailed settings.

Change: use list rows for navigation, switches for preferences, quiet controls for secondary actions and one clear submit/send action per task. Group post options into saving, timeline and safety sections. Make gift selection distinct from sending, move Favourite to a compact labelled control, and keep the selected gift/price/Send summary reachable. Give destructive actions a consistent warning treatment.

Acceptance: the primary task is immediately clear, secondary actions remain discoverable, and larger targets do not turn every repeated action into a large card. Match preview gift selection/confirmation to the existing native selection-plus-Send pattern. Keep account deletion separated and explicitly confirmed.

Sources: gift card rendering around line 517; Profile settings and native gift footer; post action rows; Settings and moderation action groups.

D09 · P2State clarity

Make status, empty states and feedback mean the same thing everywhere

Several edge states need polish. The empty library displays a Topics heading without entries. The paused preview chat tells ordinary members to enable Host settings they cannot access. Profile avatars have decorative rings even when Home has no active stories; the native own/public profile avatars also pass ring unconditionally, so the active-story visual language is ambiguous.

Concrete native source issues: FeedControls renders task errors outside the preferences Modal, so a failed action can leave feedback behind the open sheet. ModeratorSheet can render both an empty-state card and a second “No open reports” line. Errors should not coexist with a misleading empty-success state.

Change: define loading, empty, unavailable, error, success and permission-limited patterns. Put errors beside the affected field/action inside the active sheet. Show one useful empty state, identify the next permitted action, and reserve story-status rings/pulsation for unexpired stories; use a visually distinct treatment for decorative profile framing.

Acceptance: every state gives a clear explanation and an appropriate next action. No hidden errors or duplicate empty messages. No active-story implication when no story exists. Respect reduced-motion settings and the 24-hour expiry rule.

Sources: FeedControls, error placement around lines 335–339; ModeratorSheet, empty render paths around lines 1014–1023 and 1079; unconditional profile rings around lines 417 and 893; library/chat empty states; active story ring/expiry behaviour.

D10 · P2Confirmed representation gaps

Make the preview a reliable representation of the mobile app

Shared colours and some components now align, but important flows still differ. Preview discovery filters expand inline while native uses a sheet. Preview Question mode exposes poll-option fields, while native correctly makes them conditional. Preview circle search remains outside tools. Preview reporting ends in a “no report submitted” toast rather than showing the native form. The gift flow and notification settings also represent different structures.

Change: define one approved screen/state inventory and mirror layout, relevant conditional fields, labels and action hierarchy in both implementations. Keep simulation notices explicit. Consolidate state selectors so late CSS overrides cannot silently erase selected states. Add focused layout assertions for D01–D03, not just page-wide overflow.

Acceptance: compare the same fixture, permissions and state in preview and native. Sign-in/recovery, member profiles, host/moderator states, keyboard, safe-area, Dynamic Type and real store-price states need native captures before release. Preview evidence must remain labelled as such.

Sources: native DiscoveryControls; native participation fields and CircleFeatures; preview equivalents; native report/preferences screens; existing screen tests; existing layout checks. Existing passing tests do not cover the newly reproduced menu height and feed-tab wrapping defects.

Implementation action plan

  1. Resolve usability defects: D01–D03. Add focused tests for the conversation menu’s usable visible area, enlarged feed controls and distinct filter selection.
  2. Unify shared controls: D04–D06. Add semantic colour roles, normalize icons/targets and apply typography tokens without reducing reading sizes.
  3. Align screen structures: D07–D08. Stabilize circle navigation, simplify repeated secondary controls, and improve gift/settings/action organization.
  4. Complete states and parity: D09–D10. Fix hidden feedback and duplicate empty states, clarify story rings and paused-chat permissions, and mirror native conditional forms.
  5. Verify the resulting app: compare representative populated/empty/loading/error/permission states in both themes and at 320/390 px, then capture native iOS/Android with keyboard, safe areas, Dynamic Type, long names and screen-reader focus. Test the control’s own clipping and label geometry, not just the page width.

Scope and verification limits

  • No page-wide horizontal overflow was observed in the 44 captured preview states. D01 and D02 show why that alone is insufficient: internal clipping and extreme word wrapping can still make a layout unusable.
  • The preview has its own HTML/CSS implementation. Native source risks are not represented as observed device defects. Authentication, moderator, real keyboard, real store pricing and native-only permission states need device screenshots.
  • The 140% preview setting currently applies an uneven blanket 22 px rule. It is a useful stress case, not an accurate simulation of iOS Dynamic Type or Android font scaling.
  • Palettes were calculated from shared tokens and sampled controls; this is not a complete contrast, accessibility, functional or security certification.
  • Navigation and temporary preview display settings were used for inspection and restored to System/100% with the normal viewport. No post, message, invitation, report, upload, gift or purchase was submitted, and no account preference was changed.
  • The earlier layout audit and implementation record remain historical evidence. This fresh report evaluates the resulting current state and should guide the next pass.
